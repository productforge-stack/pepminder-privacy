How to Track GLP-1 Doses: Semaglutide & Tirzepatide Tracking That Actually Sticks

Most people on a GLP-1 start tracking with a notes app or a spreadsheet. It works for about a month. Then a dose gets logged twice, or you can't remember which side you injected last week, or the sheet quietly falls behind and your "data" becomes guesswork. Here's what a tracking system for a weekly (or split-dose) injectable actually needs, and how to set it up.

Note: this is about record-keeping, not treatment. Doses and titration schedules come from your prescriber, not from an app or an article.

What a GLP-1 log actually needs to capture

  1. Every dose, with a timestamp. Weekly injections make "did I take it Friday or Saturday?" a real problem five weeks in. One-tap logging beats retroactive memory.
  2. Injection site. Rotating sites is standard practice, and remembering "left abdomen or right thigh?" across weeks is exactly what memory is bad at. A site log answers it instantly.
  3. Weight over time. The number that tells you what's happening isn't any single weigh-in — it's the trend line across weeks.
  4. The vial or pen you're drawing from. If you use compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide in vials, you also need reconstitution math (mg/mL → syringe units) and the date the vial was mixed, because reconstituted vials expire.
  5. A reminder that survives being ignored. A calendar alert you swipe away is gone. A reminder you can mark Taken or Skip keeps the log honest.

Spreadsheet vs. app

A spreadsheet can hold columns for all of the above. What it can't do:

  • remind you,
  • calculate units to draw from a compounded vial,
  • warn you a reconstituted vial is past its window,
  • log a dose from your lock screen in one tap.

The tracking method that works is the one with the least friction on a random Tuesday morning. That's the whole argument for an app.

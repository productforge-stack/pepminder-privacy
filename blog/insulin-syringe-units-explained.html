Insulin Syringe Units Explained: Reading 30, 50, and 100-Unit Syringes

"Units" is the most confusing word in peptide tracking, because it sounds like an amount of compound. It isn't. On a standard U-100 insulin syringe, a unit is a volume: 1/100th of a milliliter. This guide covers how to read the three common syringe sizes and convert between mL and units.

Note: this article explains how syringe markings work. It does not recommend doses or protocols.

The one conversion you need

units = mL × 100        mL = units ÷ 100
Volume U-100 units
0.05 mL 5 units
0.1 mL 10 units
0.25 mL 25 units
0.5 mL 50 units
1.0 mL 100 units

The three common sizes

Syringe Total volume Typical marking
30-unit (0.3 mL) 0.3 mL Every 1 unit (some ½ unit)
50-unit (0.5 mL) 0.5 mL Every 1 unit
100-unit (1 mL) 1.0 mL Every 2 units

All three use the same unit scale: 10 units is 0.1 mL on every one of them. Smaller barrels spread the same volume over more physical length, so small draws are easier to read precisely on a 30-unit syringe than on a 100-unit syringe, where each tick is 2 units.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a 100-unit syringe as if every tick is 1 unit. On most 1 mL syringes each small line is 2 units.
  • Assuming the same units means the same amount. 10 units of a 2.5 mg/mL solution and 10 units of a 5 mg/mL solution contain different amounts. Units depend on how you reconstituted the vial (see our reconstitution math guide).
  • Mixing up U-100 and U-40 syringes. U-40 syringes (used in veterinary settings) have a different scale entirely. Everything here assumes U-100.
